# Write Like Human

Keep the author's intent, facts, constraints, and voice in control. Prefer clear, specific, grounded prose over generic, inflated, or overexplained writing.

## Match the Requested Edit

- **Proofread or fix a small passage:** correct the identified problem and preserve the rest. No mandatory cut, vocabulary substitution, rhythm change, or style exercise is required. If the text already meets the request, it may remain unchanged.
- **Draft or substantially rewrite:** infer purpose, audience, format, voice, language, and length from the material. Ask only about missing information that materially affects the result; proceed when context is sufficient.
- **Diagnose a particular weakness:** read the relevant section of [editorial patterns](references/editorial-patterns.md) for evidence, specificity, rhythm, voice, anti-patterns, or genre structure. Do not load or apply every section by default.
- **Review rather than rewrite:** identify concrete passages, their effect on the reader, and a proportionate suggestion. Do not replace the draft unless asked.

## Preserve Meaning and Credibility

- Start from the user's draft or notes. Preserve their claims, uncertainty, vocabulary, strongest lines, and distinctive details. Voice samples guide a substantial rewrite without requiring a formal style guide for every edit.
- Do not fabricate personal history, metrics, citations, experience, sources, or feelings. Ask for necessary evidence or mark missing details plainly.
- Respect institution or employer rules for resumes, applications, academic work, and policy-bound submissions. If AI-written final content is prohibited, offer coaching, an outline, critique, or revision suggestions instead of deceptive final copy.
- Keep claims proportional to their evidence. Avoid turning modest observations into grand conclusions or forcing emotion the genre does not call for.
- Retain sentences that add information, meaning, argument, or necessary rhythm. Remove repetition only when it does not serve the audience.

## One Proportionate Final Check

Check the requested change and the resulting text together: meaning and voice preserved, facts not invented, constraints met, and no new ambiguity introduced. For longer work, also check avoidable repetition, evidence, flow, and the ending.

Use a conditional edit when it solves an observed problem: cut generic filler if present, replace noun-heavy phrasing when a verb is clearer, vary monotonous rhythm when needed, and remove a forced conclusion if one exists. There is no quota to change at least one of anything. When shortening, preserve the details carrying voice, specificity, and proof.

If a necessary fact cannot be verified, distinguish the user's supplied claim from verified evidence, state uncertainty when it matters, or leave a labeled placeholder. Do not invent a fact to finish. If constraints conflict, identify the consequential conflict and ask for the missing decision while completing independent work.

## Deliver

Produce the requested text directly. For revisions, avoid explaining every edit unless asked. Add a brief note only for material assumptions, missing facts, limitations, or requested editorial reasoning. Completion means the agreed writing task is satisfied, not that a fixed series of stylistic transformations has been performed.
